# Infrared

The NordVPN glossary entry defines infrared (IR) as a wireless method for sending data between devices over short to mid ranges. IR has a longer wavelength than visible light and a shorter one than radio waves. People can’t see it, and only specific sensors and cameras can detect it.

The article lists several limitations of IR:

- It has a limited range.
- It needs line of sight.
- Its data transfer rate is low.
- It is prone to interference.
- It has poor penetration.
- It carries security risks.

It then describes four ways hackers can misuse IR:

- **Spoofing devices:** Infrared smart light bulbs could be used to spoof the devices on a Wi-Fi network. That could lead to data leaks or stolen details such as IP addresses.
- **Intercepting the connection:** An attacker could monitor the data two devices exchange and steal things like login credentials, messages and emails. The article says this takes advanced skills and equipment, so it’s less common than other attacks.
- **Stealing IoT data:** Many IoT devices use infrared bulbs, which attackers could exploit to get at sensitive user data.
- **Monitoring security cameras:** Intercepting IR signals could give attackers access to a company’s cameras. They could then watch what the company does and use the cameras to get into its security system.
