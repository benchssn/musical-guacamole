
**The basics.** WiFi (802.11 networking) sends data as radio waves, much like two-way radio. Your device’s wireless adapter turns data into a radio signal. A router decodes it and passes it to the internet over a wired ethernet connection. The same process runs in reverse for incoming data.

**Frequencies.**

- WiFi uses 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz, higher than cell phones, walkie-talkies and TV, so it can carry more data.
- 2.4 GHz travels farther and passes through walls better, but it is slower. It can be faster from several rooms away.
- 5 GHz is faster up close but has a shorter range (about 200 ft at best) and is more easily blocked.

**Standards, oldest to newest.**

- 802.11b (1999): 11 Mbps, 2.4 GHz.
- 802.11a: 54 Mbps, 5 GHz.
- 802.11g: 54 Mbps, 2.4 GHz.
- 802.11n (2009): much better speed and range, with multiple data streams.
- 802.11ac (~2014): 5 GHz only, much faster, also called “Gigabit WiFi”.
- 802.11ax / WiFi 6 (2019): up to 9.2 Gbps, handles many devices at once, adds a 6 GHz band.
- 802.11be / WiFi 7 (2024): better range, more connections and higher speeds.

**Hot spots.** A hot spot is any area with accessible wireless, such as airports and cafés. They can be free or paid, and you can make your own with a phone. Most modern devices have WiFi built in, and older machines can use a USB or PCI adapter.

**Building a home network.**

- A wireless router combines a modem port, router, ethernet hub, firewall and access point.
- Coverage is about 100 ft, and range extenders help in larger homes.
- You can change the network name (SSID), channel (the default is usually 6, so switch if neighbours cause interference) and security settings.

**Security.**

- WEP and the original WPA are outdated and insecure.
- WPA2 is the long-time recommended standard, and WPA3 (2018, standard since 2020) is stronger.
- Turn off WPS, which has a known vulnerability.
- MAC filtering helps but can be spoofed.
- Change the default SSID and use a strong password.
- Wired connections are safer for sensitive data.
- Attackers need to be within physical range, so home attacks are unlikely.

**Quick FAQ answers.** A router shares the modem’s internet signal with all your devices. Test your speed with a speed-test app on each device. Yes, WiFi can be hacked, so change the SSID and block WAN requests.
